Why does using ChatGPT still take me hours every week?

By Chinenye · 23 November 2026 · 2 min read

The short answer

Because you start from a blank chat every time and re-explain your business, your voice and your offers. The fix is a persistent workspace, such as a Claude Project, that already holds your brand voice, your offers and your rules. You stop briefing a stranger and start working with something that already knows you.

You open a blank chat. "I run a small business that sells..." Again.

Then your tone. Your audience. What it should not say. You paste an example. Twenty minutes later the draft sounds like everybody's draft, and you edit for another twenty.

That is not AI being slow. That is you training a stranger every single morning.

The problem is the blank page

A blank chat knows nothing about you. So every answer starts from the average of the internet, which is why so much AI writing sounds the same. I wrote about that in why everything you write with AI sounds like AI.

A workspace remembers

A persistent workspace, like a Claude Project, holds your context for you. Your brand voice. Your offers. Who you serve. The rules, like words you never use. A few examples of writing you are proud of.

Every new chat inside it starts with all of that already in place.

Say you write captions three times a week. In a blank chat you re-brief each time. In a workspace, you type "caption for Thursday's offer" and get a draft that already sounds like you.

It still needs your eyes

The first draft will be closer. It will not be perfect. You still read it, cut what is off, and add the line only you would write.

The steps

  1. Pick one recurring job. Captions, weekly emails, client replies.
  2. Write a one-page brief. Voice, audience, rules, what to avoid.
  3. Add three good examples. Real writing you like.
  4. Save it as a workspace. One workspace per job works best.
  5. Run it for a week. Note what it gets wrong and update the brief.

The Starter Kit has ready setups for this, with the prompts already written, if you would rather not start from nothing.

You would not re-introduce yourself to a colleague every morning. Abeg, do not do it to your tools.

Questions people ask

Is a Claude Project the only way?

No. Other AI tools have similar saved workspaces. The idea is what matters: save the context once.

What should I put in the workspace?

Your voice rules, your offers, who you serve, examples of writing you like, and things to never say.

  • How do I remind clients to pay invoices or finish checkout without sounding desperate?

    Take yourself out of the awkward part. Write the reminders once in a calm tone, then let a timed sequence send them: a gentle nudge, a clear reminder, and a firm final notice. The same words go to everyone, so it never feels personal, and you stop chasing money while you are annoyed.

  • How do I keep track of daily business expenses and profit without spending hours on spreadsheets?

    Stop typing the numbers yourself. Send receipts and bank alerts to one place, let a simple setup read each one and add a row to a Google Sheet, and let one formula work out profit. You check the sheet once a week instead of building it every night. The setup does the typing, you do the checking.

  • What do I need to start using AI in my business?

    Less than you think. One AI tool, one task you repeat every week, a few lines about your business and about 30 minutes. You do not need to code or buy anything first. Start with one job, get it working, then add the next.

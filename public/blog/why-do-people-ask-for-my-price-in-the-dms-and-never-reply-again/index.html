Why do people ask for my price in the DMs and never reply again?

By Chinenye · 21 November 2026 · 2 min read

The short answer

Because by the time you answered, they had already moved on. A price question is a buying signal with a short shelf life. If the reply comes hours later, the buyer has asked someone else. An automatic first reply that gives the price or a link and asks one question keeps the conversation alive while you are busy.

Someone sends: "How much?" You are packing an order, so you reply three hours later.

Silence. You never hear from them again.

So you tell yourself they were not serious. Now stay with me. Most of the time they were. They just asked somebody else while you were busy.

The shelf life of a price question

A price question is the moment a buyer is closest to buying. A few minutes later, they are comparing. A few hours later, they are distracted or have decided.

This is called speed to lead, and it is not about being rude to be slow. It is about the fact that interest cools quickly.

What an instant reply does

You do not need to answer everything at once. You need to keep the conversation warm until you can.

A good first reply does three things. It answers the question, or points to where the answer is. It gives a next step, such as a link or a booking. And it asks one easy question, so the buyer replies.

Say a customer messages about a skincare set. The reply gives the price range, a link to the product page, and asks what skin concern they are shopping for. Now they are talking, and you are still packing.

What it should not do

It should not pretend to be you in a way that lies. It should not dump a wall of text. And it should hand over to you when a conversation gets personal or complicated.

I wrote about replying to DMs at night without staying awake and what happens when customers make typos. Both matter here.

The steps

  1. Write your top five questions. Price, delivery, payment, availability, how to order.
  2. Write the answers once. Short, in your voice.
  3. Add a link and one question. Always give a next step.
  4. Set the hand-over rule. Tell the system when to stop and tag you.
  5. Test with a friend. Ask odd questions and see what breaks.

The DM automation page shows how I build this for a business.

A slow reply is a quiet no. Make it a fast hello instead.

Questions people ask

Will an automatic reply sound robotic?

It can, if it is a menu of buttons. A reply that reads the message and answers the actual question sounds much more natural.

Should I put the price in the reply?

That depends on your business. Many sellers share the price and a link, then ask what the buyer needs.

Keep reading

  • How can I take a break from my business without losing customers?

    Let the basics run without you. Automatic replies, link delivery, payment confirmation and lead capture can keep going while you rest. Decide in advance what must be answered fast, what can wait and what only you can handle. Then a weekend away stops costing you sales, and your phone stops owning you.

  • How do I stop answering the same customer questions twenty times a day?

    Write the answers once, then put them where customers ask. Turn your top ten questions into saved replies, a short FAQ page and an automatic first response that handles price, delivery, payment and order status. You still step in for anything unusual, but the repeats stop eating your day.

  • Why does my Instagram DM bot break when customers make typos?

    Most DM bots look for one exact word. If the customer writes it differently, the bot finds no match and goes silent or repeats a menu. The fix is a setup that reads the whole message, so a typo, slang or a normal sentence still gets the right answer.

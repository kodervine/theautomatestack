Why does my Instagram DM bot break when customers make typos?

By Chinenye · 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

The short answer

Most DM bots look for one exact word. If the customer writes it differently, the bot finds no match and goes silent or repeats a menu. The fix is a setup that reads the whole message, so a typo, slang or a normal sentence still gets the right answer.

Your bot is not broken. It is doing exactly what you told it to do.

You told it to wait for one word. The customer used a different one.

That is the whole problem, and it is a very common one. Here is why it happens, and what to do instead.

What is actually happening

A keyword bot works from a list. If the message contains a word from the list, it replies. If it does not, it does nothing, or it sends the same menu again.

That works in a test. You type PRICE, it answers, and you feel good.

Then real customers arrive.

What real messages look like

People type fast, on small screens, in the middle of something else.

"How much be this one."

"Price pls."

"Is it available abeg."

"Hey saw ur ad, can i pay half now"

None of those is a clean keyword. Some have the word you wanted, spelled differently. Some have no keyword at all, just a normal sentence. A rigid bot looks at each one and finds nothing.

What the customer thinks

This is the part that costs you money.

The customer does not think, "this bot has a rule problem." They think, "nobody is replying."

So they wait a moment, then message another seller. You paid for the ad that brought them to your inbox, and they left without ever knowing a rule was the reason.

Fixes that do not work

Adding more keywords. You add PRICE, then PRICES, then PRICE PLS, then PRICE OOO. It never ends, because people can always write it a new way.

Adding a menu. "Reply 1 for price, 2 for delivery." It works until someone types a sentence, and then it fails the same way. It also asks serious buyers to do work you should be doing.

Replying yourself. It works, but only while you are awake, charged and free.

What works instead

A setup that reads the whole message and works out what the customer wants. A typo does not stop it. Slang does not stop it. A normal sentence does not stop it.

It still sends your answers, in your words. It just does not need the customer to say the magic word first. And it runs on a server, so your phone battery and your power do not matter.

Why it hurts most during a sale

On a normal week, a missed message is one lost sale. During a big sale, the same rule fails again and again, with every buyer typing the way they always do. The more traffic you send, the more of it your bot drops. That is why this is worth fixing before your next push, and not after. If a sale is coming, read how to get your DMs ready too.

The steps

  1. Open your inbox and read your last twenty messages. Read what customers really sent, not what you think they send.
  2. Mark every message your bot ignored. That list is your fix list.
  3. Replace exact-word rules with a setup that reads the whole message.
  4. Keep your own answers for the important questions. Price, delivery and payment. The system uses your words.
  5. Add a checkout link and a backup payment option. If a card fails, the buyer should see another way to pay.
  6. Test with typos on purpose. Send it ten bad messages and fix whatever it misses.
  7. Decide what happens when it does not know. An honest "I will reply in the morning" beats silence.

People type like people. Build for that.

Questions people ask

What is a keyword trigger?

A rule that fires only when a message contains a chosen word, such as PRICE. If the customer writes it another way, the rule does not fire.

Do I still need keywords at all?

They are fine for starting a conversation from a comment. The trouble starts when the whole conversation depends on them.

Why do customers not just retype the word?

Most do not realise there is a rule. They think nobody is replying, and they go to another seller.

How do I test for this?

Send your setup ten messy messages on purpose, with typos, slang and half sentences. Fix whatever it misses.

How do I stop answering the same customer questions twenty times a day?

By Chinenye · 26 November 2026 · 2 min read

The short answer

Write the answers once, then put them where customers ask. Turn your top ten questions into saved replies, a short FAQ page and an automatic first response that handles price, delivery, payment and order status. You still step in for anything unusual, but the repeats stop eating your day.

"Is it still available?" "How much is delivery?" "Do you ship to Abuja?" "How do I pay?"

By lunchtime you have typed those answers many times and you are tired of your own voice.

Here is the quiet truth. Most of your messages are the same ten questions wearing different clothes.

Find the ten

Scroll your last week of messages. Write down every question that showed up more than once. Price, delivery time, delivery areas, payment method, availability, returns, order status.

Most businesses find the list is shorter than they expected. That is good news.

Write each answer once

Short, clear, and in your voice. Include the next step: a link, a form, or a question back.

Now that answer exists in one place. You can reuse it everywhere.

Put it where people ask

Three places do most of the work. Saved replies, so you can send an answer in one tap. A short FAQ on your website, so people find answers without asking. And an automatic first reply in your DMs that can answer the common ones straight away.

Say a customer asks if an item is in stock. The reply checks your list, answers yes or no, and shares the order link. You never touch it.

Keep a human in the loop

When a question does not match, the message should come to you with a tag. Better a slower answer than a wrong one.

Replying at night without staying awake goes deeper on this.

The steps

  1. Collect a week of questions. Copy them into one list.
  2. Group the repeats. Aim for ten.
  3. Write the answers. One short reply each.
  4. Add the next step. A link or a question.
  5. Set up saved replies. Then a basic FAQ page.
  6. Automate the first response. Only for the safe ones.

Save your voice for the new questions. The old ones can answer themselves.

Questions people ask

How many questions should I start with?

Ten. They usually cover most of what customers ask.

What about questions I have never seen before?

The system should hand them to you. Do not let it guess on anything it does not know.

Keep reading

  • How can I take a break from my business without losing customers?

    Let the basics run without you. Automatic replies, link delivery, payment confirmation and lead capture can keep going while you rest. Decide in advance what must be answered fast, what can wait and what only you can handle. Then a weekend away stops costing you sales, and your phone stops owning you.

  • Why do people ask for my price in the DMs and never reply again?

    Because by the time you answered, they had already moved on. A price question is a buying signal with a short shelf life. If the reply comes hours later, the buyer has asked someone else. An automatic first reply that gives the price or a link and asks one question keeps the conversation alive while you are busy.

  • Why does my Instagram DM bot break when customers make typos?

    Most DM bots look for one exact word. If the customer writes it differently, the bot finds no match and goes silent or repeats a menu. The fix is a setup that reads the whole message, so a typo, slang or a normal sentence still gets the right answer.

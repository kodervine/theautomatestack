Should I hire a virtual assistant for admin work, or is there a cheaper way?

By Chinenye · 20 November 2026 · 2 min read

The short answer

Automate the repetitive, rule-based tasks first: data entry, reminders, invoices and follow-ups. Hire a person for judgment, relationships and anything that changes every time. If you hire first, you pay a human to do robot work and you spend weeks explaining a process that is not written down yet.

You are overwhelmed, so you think: I need an assistant.

Fair. But before you hire one, ask what you would hand over on day one.

If the answer is "copy this from here to there, send the same message, update the sheet", you are about to pay a person to do robot work. And you will have to teach them first.

The test

For every task, ask: are the steps the same every time?

If yes, software can probably do it. Data entry, reminders, sending an invoice, a first reply to a price question, saving a file.

If no, if it needs taste, a judgment call or a tricky conversation, that is where a human earns their pay.

The hidden cost of hiring first

Say you hire someone and hand them your admin. You now have to explain how your business works. Where the sheet is. How you name things. What to say to a client who is late.

If that process lives only in your head, you will spend weeks explaining it. And the first time you change how you do something, you explain it again.

Writing the process down so software can run it does the same job as training, and you can reuse it.

When hiring is the right call

Hire when the work needs a person. Managing clients, creating things, handling complaints, making decisions when the answer is unclear.

It also helps to hire into a clean setup. A new assistant in a business with written steps and automated basics gets useful much faster.

The steps

  1. List your tasks for two days. Everything, even the small ones.
  2. Mark the repeaters. Same steps every time.
  3. Automate the top two or three. Start where you lose the most time.
  4. Write down the rest. A short checklist is enough.
  5. Then decide about hiring. What is left is the real job description.

Not sure which tasks to start with? Here is how I would pick the first one.

Hire for judgment. Automate the rest. Your future assistant will thank you.

Questions people ask

Does this mean never hire anyone?

No. It means hire for the work that needs judgment, and hand the repeatable steps to software first.

What if I do not know which tasks repeat?

Write down everything you do for two days. The tasks you do the same way each time are the ones to automate.

    Because small chores add up. Answering five price questions, hunting for a receipt, sending a payment link and updating a sheet each take two minutes, so they feel harmless.

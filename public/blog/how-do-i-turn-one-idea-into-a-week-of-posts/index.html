How do I turn one idea into a week of posts?

By Chinenye · 9 October 2026 · 3 min read

The short answer

Start with one lesson you already teach, in three to six sentences. Give it to an AI tool with your voice and a rule not to invent anything, and ask for one newsletter, three short captions and three hooks. Edit them, add one real example, and spread them across your week.

You do not have a content problem. You have one good lesson and no time.

That is usually the whole story. You know something useful. You have said it to a client, in a voice note, in a DM. But when it is time to post, you stare at the screen and start from zero.

So here is a better way to start.

Start with a lesson, not a topic

A topic is "bookkeeping". A lesson is "keep one account for your business and nothing else".

The tool cannot make up your lesson for you. It can only reshape what you give it. Thin input gives thin posts. So the first job is yours: write or say one lesson you really teach, in three to six sentences.

Say you run a bookkeeping service for small shops. Your lesson might be: "Most owners mix business and personal spending in one account. Then at the end of the month nobody knows what the shop really earned. I tell them to open a separate account and move only what they pay themselves." That is a view, a rule and a reason. It is enough to work with.

If typing feels slow, speak it into a voice note and turn it into text. Your own rhythm is in there.

What you ask for

You are not asking for a week of posts. You are asking for a small set of things that come from one idea:

  • one newsletter draft
  • three short captions
  • three hook options for a short video

Give the tool your lesson, tell it to use your voice and plain words, and tell it not to invent stories, numbers or client results. That last rule matters more than the rest.

Where the week comes from

This is the part people miss. One lesson, cut three ways, fills a week without repeating yourself.

A caption on Monday states the rule. A short video on Wednesday opens with a hook and shows the mistake. The newsletter on Friday tells the longer story. Same idea. Different shape each time.

Edit before you post

Do not skip this.

Read each piece out loud. Cross out anything you would never say to a friend. Cross out any claim you have not really made. Then add one real example from a real client or a real day, because that is the line no tool can write for you.

Three ways this goes wrong

You paste a topic. "Bookkeeping" gives you a brochure. A lesson gives you a post.

You post it as it came. The first version is a rough cut. Treat it like one.

You let it add a story. If it did not happen, it does not go in. A made-up result costs you far more than a quiet week.

The steps

  1. Pick one lesson you already teach. Not a topic. A lesson.
  2. Write or speak it in three to six sentences.
  3. Paste it in with your voice and your rules. Plain words, and no invented stories, numbers or results.
  4. Ask for a newsletter draft, three short captions and three hook options.
  5. Edit all of it. Cross out what you would not say. Add one real example.
  6. Spread it across your week. Caption first, video in the middle, newsletter at the end.
  7. Post one edited hook and watch the response. Change one thing next time and compare.

I would rather post one lesson three ways than three half-ideas once.

Questions people ask

How long does this take?

About ten minutes to set up and run it, plus however long you spend editing. The editing is the part you should not skip.

Can I speak the lesson instead of typing it?

Yes. Record a voice note, turn it into text, and paste that in. Your own rhythm comes through better when you speak.

Which AI tool should I use?

It works in Claude or ChatGPT. What matters most is the lesson you paste in, not the tool.

What if the AI adds a story that did not happen?

Cross it out. Never post a made-up result or a story you cannot stand behind.

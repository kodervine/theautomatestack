Most people's record of what they earn is a folder of screenshots and a promise.

A payment comes in. You say you will write it down later. Later becomes a pile of alerts in your email and a few numbers in your Notes app. Then someone asks how business has been, and you say "fine" because you cannot say a number.

The fix is not a better app. It is deciding, once, what a record of a payment actually needs.

![The five things to write down for every payment: the date, the amount, who paid, what it was for, and how it came in.](/blog/images/what-should-i-write-down-every-time-a-customer-pays-me/five-things.png)

## The five things

Every payment gets one row, and every row has the same five things.

1. **The date.** The day the money arrived, not the day you remembered it.
2. **The amount.** In naira, as it appears in your alert.
3. **Who paid.** A first name or an order number. Not an account number.
4. **What it was for.** Two or three words. "Braids deposit". "Order 118". "Logo balance".
5. **How it came in.** Bank transfer, Selar, cash. One word.

That is the whole list. If you add a sixth column, you will stop filling it in by week two.

## Why only five

Because the record has to survive a bad week.

Say you run a small shop and Friday is crazy. Forty messages, six orders, a customer who wants a refund. Which version of your record is still up to date on Saturday? The one with five short boxes you can fill without thinking, or the one with eleven columns you meant to get back to?

Keep it short enough that it costs you seconds, not an evening.

## Keep the account numbers out

Your bank alert has more in it than your sheet needs. It carries account numbers, full names and references.

You do not need any of that to know what you made. Write the first name or the order number and move on. The less personal detail sits in your sheet, the less there is to worry about if you ever share it, or paste any of it into a tool.

## What it looks like when it works

Someone in my community sent me this after Claude automated a pile of receipts they had received from a client:

"It automated the 70 receipts I got from my client. This is something that usually takes me 3 days if I'm lazy."

Seventy receipts is a lot of rows. A record that looks the same every time is easy to hand over. One that changes shape every week is not.

That is what a good record is. Not clever. Boring and consistent.

## The steps

1. **Open one sheet and give it five columns.** Date, amount, who, what for, how.
2. **Add a sixth only for money out.** Mark each row as in or out, so refunds and costs live in the same place.
3. **Fill it from your alerts, not from memory.** The alert is your receipt.
4. **Use a first name or an order number.** Never an account number.
5. **Update once a week.** Pick a day and keep it.
6. **Look at the total at the end of the month.** If you cannot find it in a minute, the record needs fewer columns, not more.

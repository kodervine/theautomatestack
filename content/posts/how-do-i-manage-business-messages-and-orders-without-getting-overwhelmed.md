Fifty unread chats. Someone asked for a price. Someone said they paid. Someone needs delivery on Friday. Someone has gone quiet.

You scroll, trying to piece it together. By the time you are done you have forgotten why you opened the app.

A chat app is a good place to talk. It is a terrible place to run a business.

![Three steps: an order arrives in a chat, it is captured as a row with who, what and paid status, the chat is only for talking.](/blog/images/how-do-i-manage-business-messages-and-orders-without-getting-overwhelmed/orders-out-of-chats.png)

## Split talking from tracking

Talking is messy and human. Tracking should be clean and the same every time.

When an order comes in through a message, it gets captured as a row: who, what, how much, paid or not, delivery details, date. After that, you never need to scroll back to find it.

## How it can be automatic

A form or a short flow can collect the details as the customer chats. A setup adds them to a sheet. Payment confirmation updates the status. A reminder shows what still needs delivery.

Say you sell snacks and take orders on Instagram. By evening you open one sheet and see ten orders, seven paid, three to chase. No scrolling.

## Keep the chat for what it is good at

Questions, reassurance, a friendly note. That is where you are most human. Do that in chat and keep everything else outside.

For the money side, what to write down every time a customer pays is a good first read.

## The steps

1. **Design one order sheet.** Customer, item, amount, paid, delivery, date.
2. **Collect details with a form.** Link it in the chat.
3. **Add rows automatically.** Zapier, Make or n8n.
4. **Update status on payment.** From your payment tool or alert.
5. **Check a daily view.** Unpaid and undelivered.
6. **Keep chats for conversation.** Nothing else.

Your memory is not a database. Stop using it as one.

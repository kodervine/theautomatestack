You put money behind a post. The likes climbed. Comments came in. Nobody bought.

That is a very normal result, and it does not mean ads do not work for you. It usually means the ad was asked to do the wrong job.

![Three steps: a boosted post gets likes, there is no clear next step, so use one goal, one offer and a fast reply.](/blog/images/i-boosted-a-post-and-got-likes-but-no-sales-what-went-wrong/likes-vs-sales.png)

## Likes and buying are different things

When you boost a post with no clear goal, the platform tries to get what it can easily count, and that is usually attention. People who like content like it. That is not the same as people who are ready to pay.

## Give the ad one job

Choose the one thing you want a person to do. Message you. Click to your page. Book a call. Put that in the post in plain words: "Send us a message to get the price."

Say you sell hair products. The post shows one product, the price range, and one line: "Message us to order." Now a like is not the goal. A message is.

## Be ready when they reply

If someone messages and waits three hours, you lose most of them. I wrote about [what happens to ad spend when you reply too slowly](/blog/what-happens-to-my-black-friday-ad-spend-if-i-reply-too-slowly/). An automatic first reply fixes a lot of that.

## The steps

1. **Pick one goal.** Messages, link clicks or bookings.
2. **Pick one offer.** One product or one service, not your whole shop.
3. **Say the next step in the post.** In plain words.
4. **Reply fast.** Use a saved or automatic first reply.
5. **Count what matters.** Messages and payments, not likes.

Attention is cheap. Buyers are the point.

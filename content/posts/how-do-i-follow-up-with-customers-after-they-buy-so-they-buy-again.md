The sale is done. The money landed. Most people then move on to the next customer and never speak to this one again.

That is where repeat business is lost.

![Three steps: a thank you and delivery check, ask how it went, share something useful or new.](/blog/images/how-do-i-follow-up-with-customers-after-they-buy-so-they-buy-again/after-sale-sequence.png)

## A small sequence is enough

Day of purchase: a thank you with what to expect. A few days later: did it arrive, is everything fine? A week or two later: how is it going? Then, weeks after, something useful or new.

Say a customer buys a hair set. The follow-up asks how her hair is doing and shares a short tip on caring for it. That is service, and it is also a quiet reminder of you.

## Make it about them

If every message says "buy again", people stop reading. A tip, a check-in, a heads-up on restock. The sale comes from being remembered kindly.

## Automate it

Write the four messages once. A workflow sends them based on the purchase date. You answer replies yourself.

This pairs well with [what should happen the second a client pays you](/blog/what-should-happen-the-second-a-client-pays-you/).

## The steps

1. **Write four short messages.**
2. **Set the timing.** Day 0, day 3, day 10, day 30.
3. **Connect it to your payments.**
4. **Reply personally to responses.**
5. **Adjust what gets no reply.**

Repeat customers are built after the sale, not before it.

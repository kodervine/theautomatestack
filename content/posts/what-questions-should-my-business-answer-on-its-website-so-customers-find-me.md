Your DMs are a goldmine, and most people treat them as a nuisance.

Every message asking "how much?" or "do you deliver?" is a customer telling you exactly what they want to know. And somebody else is typing the same thing into Google right now.

![Three steps: collect the questions you get asked, answer each in plain words, publish them as pages and FAQs.](/blog/images/what-questions-should-my-business-answer-on-its-website-so-customers-find-me/questions-to-answer.png)

## The usual suspects

Price. How it works. Where you are, or how delivery works. How to pay. What happens if something goes wrong. Who it suits and who it does not.

## Go and collect them

Scroll the last month of messages. Write down every question that came up twice or more. That is your list.

## Answer like you talk

Three or four plain sentences each. Not marketing. Say the price range if you can, say what is included, say what to do next.

## Publish them where they can be found

A FAQ section on your site, and where it deserves its own page, a full post. [This is how I think about turning repeat questions into answers](/blog/how-do-i-stop-answering-the-same-customer-questions-twenty-times-a-day/).

## The steps

1. **Export or scroll a month of messages.**
2. **List the repeat questions.**
3. **Write a short honest answer to each.**
4. **Put them on your site.**
5. **Add new ones each month.**

Answer once, and let the answer work for you.

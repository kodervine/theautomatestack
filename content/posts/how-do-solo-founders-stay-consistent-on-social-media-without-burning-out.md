Posting on three platforms is a full-time job nobody is paying you for.

So you post for a week, get tired, disappear, feel guilty, and start again. Low-key a cycle, abi?

I am not judging. But the problem is not discipline. You are creating the same idea from scratch three times.

![One idea or voice note branches into an Instagram caption, a LinkedIn post and a newsletter paragraph.](/blog/images/how-do-solo-founders-stay-consistent-on-social-media-without-burning-out/one-idea-many-posts.png)

## One idea, said once

Pick one idea a week. Something you actually know. A mistake a client makes. A question you keep getting. Say it out loud as a voice note, or write five rough lines.

That is the only part that needs you.

## Let the rest be drafted

A connected setup takes that one idea and drafts the pieces. A caption for Instagram. A longer post for LinkedIn. A paragraph for your email. Each in the right shape for the place it goes.

Say you give it a voice note about why clients forget to send their documents. You get three drafts. You fix the wording, add one line of your own, and schedule them in one sitting.

I wrote about [turning one idea into a week of posts](/blog/how-do-i-turn-one-idea-into-a-week-of-posts/) and [planning a month in one sitting](/blog/how-do-i-plan-a-month-of-instagram-posts-in-one-sitting/). This is the same method.

## What to watch

Do not let it post for you without checking. AI can drift off your voice. A weekly read-through keeps it honest and keeps your name safe.

## The steps

1. **Choose one idea a week.** Something you really know.
2. **Capture it quickly.** A voice note or five lines.
3. **Draft the formats.** Caption, post, email paragraph.
4. **Edit in one block.** Thirty minutes, once a week.
5. **Schedule it all together.** A scheduler such as Buffer works well.
6. **Keep a list of ideas.** So next week is never a blank page.

Consistency is easier when you only have to be creative once.

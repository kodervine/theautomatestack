You paid for the ad. Fifteen people messaged. Four bought. What happened to the other eleven?

Most businesses never find out, because nobody followed up.

![Three steps: log who asked and about what, follow up after a day or two, stop when they say no.](/blog/images/what-should-i-do-with-people-who-messaged-from-my-ad-but-did-not-buy/ad-followup.png)

## Most people need a second nudge

People get busy, wait for payday or forget. A message a day or two later brings some of them back. It is not begging. It is being helpful.

## Make it easy to remember

Log each person who messages: name, what they asked, date. A sheet is enough. Then schedule a follow-up. A workflow can send it, or remind you to.

I wrote the full method in [how to follow up with people who messaged but did not buy](/blog/how-do-i-follow-up-with-people-who-messaged-but-did-not-buy/).

## What to say

Keep it short. Refer to what they asked. Offer help. "Hi Ada, you asked about the 50ml set. Still interested? I can send the order link."

## Know when to stop

If they say no or ignore two messages, let it go. Pushing damages your name.

## The steps

1. **Log every ad message.**
2. **Write one friendly follow-up.**
3. **Schedule it for a day or two later.**
4. **Add a second, softer one if needed.**
5. **Stop when they decline.**

The money you spent on the ad is only wasted if you let the lead go cold.

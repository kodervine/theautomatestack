Chasing money is the part of business nobody enjoys. You do the work, you send the invoice, and then you wait and wonder how to ask.

The best fix is to need to ask less.

![Three steps: clear terms up front, invoice and pay link sent at once, reminders before and after the due date.](/blog/images/how-do-i-get-customers-to-pay-on-time-without-chasing-them/pay-on-time.png)

## Say it before the work

State the price, what is included, when payment is due and how to pay, before you begin. A short message is enough. People rarely argue with terms they agreed to.

## Make paying easy

Send the invoice the same day with a link or clear details. The easier it is to pay, the sooner people do.

## Reminders do the awkward part

A friendly reminder before the due date, a clear one after, a firm one later. Written once, sent automatically. See [how to chase invoices without sounding rude](/blog/how-do-i-chase-unpaid-invoices-without-sounding-rude/) and [how to remind without sounding desperate](/blog/how-do-i-remind-clients-to-pay-invoices-without-sounding-desperate/).

## Ask for a deposit when you can

For custom or larger work, a deposit shows commitment and protects your time.

## The steps

1. **Write your payment terms once.**
2. **Send them before starting.**
3. **Invoice the same day.**
4. **Include a pay link.**
5. **Schedule reminders.**
6. **Stop them on payment.**

You are not chasing. You are running a process.

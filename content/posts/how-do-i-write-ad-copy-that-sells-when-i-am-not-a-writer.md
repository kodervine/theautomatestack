You open the ad box and stare at it. What do you even write?

Most people think good ad copy is clever. It is not. It is clear.

![Three steps: name one problem, say what you offer in plain words, end with one action.](/blog/images/how-do-i-write-ad-copy-that-sells-when-i-am-not-a-writer/plain-ad-copy.png)

## Three lines do most of the work

First line: the problem, in the words your customer uses. "Tired of waking up to unanswered messages?"

Second line: what you offer, in plain words. "We reply to your customers for you, day and night."

Third line: one action. "Message us to see how."

## Where AI helps

Ask it for five versions of each line, in a plain tone. Pick the one that sounds like you and trim it. If you have a workspace set up with your voice, the drafts come out closer. I wrote about that in [why using ChatGPT still takes hours](/blog/why-does-using-chatgpt-still-take-me-hours-every-week/).

## Keep it honest

Do not claim results you cannot back up, like earnings or guaranteed outcomes. Say what the product does and who it is for. It protects your name and keeps your ad account safe.

## The steps

1. **Pick one customer.** Picture them.
2. **Write their problem in their words.**
3. **Say your offer in one line.**
4. **Add one action.**
5. **Ask AI for five versions.** Choose and edit one.
6. **Read it aloud.** Cut anything that sounds like a brochure.

Plain words sell to plain people. That is most of your market.

Client one needs captions. Client two needs the carousel designed. Client three wants everything scheduled by Monday morning.

You have more than one of them. Each has a different tone, different brand colours and different posting times. You jump between accounts and tools and a spreadsheet, and by Thursday you cannot say what is done.

Then a client messages: "Is this week's content ready?"

It is not. You say it is almost done.

The problem is not your speed. It is that nothing shows you where each post is.

![One calendar, every client's posts, each with a status from idea to scheduled.](/blog/images/how-do-i-keep-track-of-content-for-several-clients/one-calendar.png)

## One calendar, one row per post

Put every client's content in one calendar. One row for each post. Give every row the same columns:

- the client
- the platform
- the date it goes out
- the hook
- the caption
- the design
- a status

The status is the part that changes your week. Idea. Drafted. Designed. Approved. Scheduled.

Now "is it ready?" is not a feeling. You filter by client and look at the statuses. If anything is not green, it is not ready, and you know which one.

## Keep each client's voice in a note

Five clients means five voices. If you carry them in your head, they blur. A client who is formal starts to sound casual, and a client who hates exclamation marks gets three.

Write a short note for each client. How they talk. Words they like. Words they never use. One example caption they loved. Keep it next to their rows and hand it to the AI every time you draft for them.

## Where AI fits

AI is good at the first draft. Hooks, captions, a version for each platform. It is not good at knowing your client.

So the order matters. It drafts, you read, you change what is not right, and you approve. Only then does the status change to approved.

That is how the Content Calendar setup works. Claude plans and writes the hooks and captions, makes the designs in Canva, and fills your Notion calendar. And nothing is ever posted for you. You approve every one.

## What it looks like for someone using it

Someone in my community connected their apps and told me this:

"Seeing the scheduled posts without opening Instagram. I was wowed."

That is what a calendar like this is for. The posts are planned and scheduled before you open the app.

## The steps

1. **Create one calendar for all your clients.**
2. **Add one row per post with the same columns.** Client, platform, date, hook, caption, design, status.
3. **Write a voice note for each client.** A few lines, one example caption.
4. **Plan one client at a time.** Fill their week in one sitting.
5. **Let AI draft the hooks and captions.** Give it the client's voice note.
6. **Read every draft and fix it.** Cross out anything the client would not say.
7. **Change the status as each post moves.** Drafted, designed, approved, scheduled.
8. **Before you reply to a client, look at the statuses.**

The [Content Calendar setup](/setups/content-pipeline/) is this, already built in Notion, with the skill that plans, writes and designs, and a test you can run on one client first.

A client does not need you to be faster. They need you to know where their content is.

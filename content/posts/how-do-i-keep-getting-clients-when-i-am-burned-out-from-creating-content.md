You are tired of posting. Every day you create something, and by evening you are not sure it did anything.

And the worst part: the day you rest, the enquiries slow down.

That tells you your content has no engine behind it. It only works while you push it.

![Three steps: post with a clear next step, a keyword or link captures the lead, a follow-up nurtures them during quiet weeks.](/blog/images/how-do-i-keep-getting-clients-when-i-am-burned-out-from-creating-content/posts-that-keep-working.png)

## Give every post a next step

A post that ends with nothing is a post that gets liked and forgotten. Add a next step. A keyword to comment. A link in the bio. A question to answer in the DMs.

Now someone can raise a hand.

## Capture the hand

When someone comments the keyword, an automatic reply sends them what you promised and asks one question. Their name and interest go into a sheet or your email list.

Say a post about weekly planning gets fifty comments over a month. Instead of fifty people drifting away, fifty get a reply and a link, and you can follow up with the ones who clicked.

## Keep them warm

A short follow-up a few days later keeps the conversation alive. [Here is how I think about following up with people who messaged but did not buy](/blog/how-do-i-follow-up-with-people-who-messaged-but-did-not-buy/).

## Reuse the best posts

Your best three posts from last quarter still work. Share them again with a new first line. Then the engine runs even on weeks when you cannot create.

## The steps

1. **Pick your best five posts.** The ones that got replies.
2. **Add a clear next step to each.** Keyword, link or question.
3. **Set the automatic reply.** A short answer and a link.
4. **Log every lead.** One sheet, one row each.
5. **Schedule a follow-up.** Two or three days later.
6. **Repost the winners.** With a new hook.

The [DM automation page](/dm-automation/) explains how the capture part works.

Content should start conversations. It should not take your weekends.

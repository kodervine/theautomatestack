Your auto-reply is the first thing a Black Friday buyer reads. So make it do something.

Most auto-replies say "Thanks for your message, we will get back to you shortly." That does nothing. The buyer already knows you got the message. What they want to know is simple: is it available, how much is it, and how do I pay.

![A good first reply does three things: it answers the question they asked, it gives the next step such as the link, and it sounds like you, not a form letter.](/blog/images/what-should-my-black-friday-auto-reply-say/good-first-reply.png)

## What a good first reply does

Three things, and only three.

It answers the question the buyer actually asked. It gives the next step, like the checkout link. And it sounds like you.

That is the whole job. A reply that does those three things, in seconds, beats a long reply that arrives tomorrow.

## Replies you can copy

Say you run a small shoe brand. These are the questions you will get most, with a reply for each. Swap the brackets for your own details.

**"Is it still available?"**

> Yes, it is available. Which size do you need? Tell me and I will send you the checkout link.

**"How much is this one?"**

> It is ₦[your price] for Black Friday. It comes in [sizes or colours]. Want me to send the checkout link?

**"Can I pay half now?"**

> Yes. You can pay [half] now and the balance by [date]. Here is how it works: [one short line]. Shall I send the link?

**"When will it arrive?"**

> We deliver to [your area]. It usually arrives in [number] days. For Black Friday, your last order date for delivery is [date].

**When you do not know the answer:**

> Thanks for asking. I do not want to guess, so I will reply properly in the morning.

Read those again. Each one answers, then moves the buyer forward. None of them is a menu.

## Put your sale details in

On Black Friday, buyers want to know when the sale ends and when they must order to get their item in time. So say it. Put your end date and your delivery cut-off in the replies where it fits, before the buyer has to ask.

## What to leave out

**A menu of numbers.** "Reply 1 for price, 2 for delivery" breaks the moment someone types a normal sentence. Buyers type like people, with typos and slang.

**A long paragraph.** If it needs a scroll on a phone, shorten it.

**A promise you cannot keep.** If you say "we reply in minutes", make sure you do.

## Test before the sale

Send your replies ten messy messages. "Price pls." "Abeg, is it available?" Half sentences. If a reply misses, fix the reply, not the buyer.

## The steps

1. **List the five questions you get most.** Copy them from your real messages.
2. **Write one short reply for each.** Answer, then give the next step.
3. **Add your checkout link and your sale details.** End date and delivery cut-off.
4. **Write the "I do not know" reply.** Honest, and promising a proper answer.
5. **Set it up so the replies go out without you.** A system that reads the whole message, not a menu.
6. **Test it with ten messy messages.** Fix whatever it misses.

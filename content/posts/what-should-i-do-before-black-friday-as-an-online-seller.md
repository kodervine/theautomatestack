Do not start with the ads. Start with the boring list.

Most Black Friday trouble does not come from a bad offer. It comes from small things nobody checked: a checkout link that fails, a delivery date nobody confirmed, a stock count that was wrong. These are all things you can fix now, while it is quiet.

Black Friday is on 27 November this year. Work through this list in order.

## Now: decide your offer and count your stock

Say you run a small bag brand. The first job is not a photo or a caption. It is a note that says what is on sale, what is not, when it ends and your last day for delivery.

- [ ] Write down your offer in one place
- [ ] Check your numbers, so you do not sell at a loss
- [ ] Count your stock, item by item
- [ ] Decide what you will do when something sells out

Everything else depends on this note. Your ads, your captions and your replies will all repeat it.

## Four weeks out: test payment and delivery

This is the step people skip, and it is the one that costs the most on the day.

- [ ] Buy something from your own checkout, start to finish
- [ ] Add a backup way to pay, like bank transfer details, for when a card fails
- [ ] Confirm you can see every payment as it lands
- [ ] Ask your delivery partner what they can handle on a busy day, and set your delivery cut-off from their answer

If something breaks here, you have weeks to fix it. If it breaks on Black Friday, you have minutes.

## Two weeks out: plan your content and ready your DMs

You will be busy that week. So write your posts before it starts.

- [ ] Plan the posts for the whole week, including a few teasers before the sale
- [ ] Write the answers to the questions buyers will ask
- [ ] Set up a way to send those answers without you
- [ ] Decide what happens when a buyer asks something you did not plan for

For the content, you can plan a month in one sitting, and the Create layer of the [Starter Kit](/starter-kit/) is built for that. For the messages, read [how to get your DMs ready](/blog/how-do-i-get-my-instagram-dms-ready-for-black-friday/), then check [what slow replies cost your ad spend](/blog/what-happens-to-my-black-friday-ad-spend-if-i-reply-too-slowly/) before you raise any budget.

## The week of: run a full test, then plan to rest

- [ ] Send your setup ten messy messages: typos, slang, half sentences
- [ ] Walk through the buyer's whole path one more time, from message to payment
- [ ] Decide who answers what, and when you will sleep

That last one matters more than it sounds. A tired seller makes slow, short replies. Plan your rest the way you plan your stock.

## After: follow up

Black Friday does not end when the sale does.

- [ ] Thank the people who bought
- [ ] Write to the people who messaged and did not buy
- [ ] Write down what worked while you still remember it

The people who asked about your product are the warmest list you will have all year. Do not let them go cold.

## The steps, in short

1. **Write your offer down and count your stock.**
2. **Test your checkout and your backup payment.**
3. **Confirm delivery and set your cut-off.**
4. **Plan your content and your DM answers.**
5. **Run a full test the week before.**
6. **Plan your rest.**
7. **Follow up after.**

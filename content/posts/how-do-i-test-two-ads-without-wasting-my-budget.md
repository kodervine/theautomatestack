You have two ideas for an ad. Which one is better? You could run both and see.

Most small businesses do this badly, and it costs them. They change the image, the words, the price and the audience all at once. Then one wins and they cannot say why.

![Three steps: same offer and budget, one difference between the ads, keep the one that paid more.](/blog/images/how-do-i-test-two-ads-without-wasting-my-budget/two-ad-test.png)

## One difference only

Keep the offer, the budget and the audience the same. Change one thing. The first line. Or the picture.

If ad B wins, you know the first line mattered. Next time you test something else.

## Same budget, same time

Give both the same amount and the same days. Otherwise you are comparing a race where one runner started late.

## Compare payments, not clicks

Say ad A got 14 messages and 2 payments. Ad B got 8 messages and 4 payments. B is better, even if A looked busier. This is why [tagging each ad](/blog/how-do-i-know-which-ad-actually-made-me-sales/) matters.

## The steps

1. **Pick one thing to test.**
2. **Make two versions.** Everything else identical.
3. **Same budget and days.**
4. **Tag each ad.**
5. **Count payments.**
6. **Keep the winner.** Test the next thing.

Slow and clear beats fast and confused.

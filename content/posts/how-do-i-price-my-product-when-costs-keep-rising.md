Costs go up. Stock, delivery, packaging, data. You keep your prices the same because you are afraid to lose customers, and your profit quietly shrinks.

The way out is to know your numbers, so a price change is a decision, not a panic.

![Three steps: list every cost per item, add the profit you need, review on a set schedule.](/blog/images/how-do-i-price-my-product-when-costs-keep-rising/price-from-costs.png)

## Know what one item really costs

Stock, delivery, packaging, payment fees, a share of ads and data, and your own time. Most people forget half of these.

Say you sell a product for ₦10,000 and it costs ₦6,500 once everything is counted. You keep ₦3,500, not ₦10,000 minus the stock. (These numbers only show the sum. Use your own.)

## Add the profit you need

Decide what you must keep per item to pay yourself and grow. Price from that, not from what competitors do.

## Review on a schedule

Check prices monthly or every quarter. When your costs move, the sheet tells you whether to adjust.

## Tell people calmly

A short note with a date. "From the 1st, the price changes to..." Fair customers understand. Reply to questions plainly.

## The steps

1. **List every cost per item.**
2. **Put it in a sheet.**
3. **Add your target profit.**
4. **Compare to your price.**
5. **Review on a schedule.**
6. **Announce changes early.**

A price you cannot defend with numbers will always feel risky.

You had a busy month. Messages all day, orders going out, alerts landing on your phone. So you would think you made money.

Then the month ends and your account does not look like it. Where did it go?

That is the question the numbers coming in will never answer on their own. Money coming in tells you how busy you were. It does not tell you what you kept.

![Money in, minus money out, equals what you kept. Track both in the same sheet.](/blog/images/how-do-i-know-if-i-am-actually-making-a-profit/in-minus-out.png)

## The one sum

What you kept is money in, minus money out.

That is all it is. The hard part is not the sum. It is that most people only write down one side. The payments get recorded because they feel good. The spending does not, so it stays in your head, or in a dozen alerts you never look at again.

Put both in the same sheet and the sum does itself.

## A made-up month

Say you sell skincare online. In one month, ₦400,000 comes in. That feels like a good month.

Now the other side. ₦180,000 went on stock. ₦45,000 on delivery. ₦30,000 on ads. ₦20,000 on data and airtime.

That is ₦275,000 out. You kept ₦125,000.

Still fine. But it is a very different number from ₦400,000, and if you had only been tracking what came in, you would have planned your next month around the wrong one.

(These numbers are only to show the sum. Use your own.)

## Where the leaks hide

When people finally put both sides together, the surprise is usually small things, repeated.

Delivery you did not charge for. Ads that did not bring sales. Data bundles. A tool you forgot you were paying for. None of them looks big on its own. Together they are the gap between busy and profitable.

Group your money out into a few simple labels and the monthly picture shows you where it went: stock, delivery, ads, tools, transport, other.

## What it looks like when it does it for you

A founding member in my community sent me this after connecting Claude to their Google Sheet:

"It worked. It updated the sheet and my sheet dashboard too."

The dashboard updated with the sheet. That is the point of keeping both sides in one place: the numbers you need are one glance away.

## The steps

1. **Put money in and money out in one sheet.** One row per payment or spend.
2. **Label every money-out row.** Stock, delivery, ads, tools, transport, other.
3. **Record spending the same week it happens.** Not at the end of the month.
4. **At the month end, add up both sides.**
5. **Subtract.** What is left is what you kept.
6. **Look at your biggest label.** That is where to look first.
7. **Keep your own pay on one line.** So your business number stays clean.

You buy data for the business. Then lunch. Then a gift for your mother. Then stock. All from the same account.

Now a customer asks if you can supply a bigger order and you cannot tell what the business has.

![Three steps: open a separate business account, pay business costs from it only, move your pay across on a schedule.](/blog/images/how-do-i-separate-business-money-from-personal-money/two-accounts.png)

## Mixed money hides the truth

When business and personal spending share an account, you cannot tell if the business made money. You only see a balance, and it tells you nothing.

## Separate first, tidy later

Open a second account for the business. Even a basic one. All business money goes in. All business costs come out of it.

## Pay yourself from it

On your pay day, move your fixed amount to your personal account. That one transfer is the only link between the two.

## If you already mixed them

Do not try to untangle the past. Pick a date. From that day, the business has its own account. It will be messy for a short while and then clear.

## The steps

1. **Open a business account.**
2. **Move business payments into it.**
3. **Pay business costs from it.**
4. **Pay yourself on a set day.**
5. **Never pay a personal bill from it.**

Clean accounts are the cheapest advice I can give you.
